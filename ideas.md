# Tea Website Design Direction

## Three Initial Approaches

### Theme Name: Quiet Orchard
**Very Brief Intro:** A sun-washed editorial tea shop inspired by orchard tables, linen, and botanical field notes. The experience feels calm, tactile, and quietly premium.
**Probability:** 0.07

### Theme Name: Night Steep
**Very Brief Intro:** A dark, atmospheric tea ritual brand with ink, copper, and precise luminous accents. The experience feels intimate, contemporary, and slightly cinematic.
**Probability:** 0.03

### Theme Name: Market Day
**Very Brief Intro:** A lively, hand-printed tea market with block colors, paper textures, and playful product storytelling. The experience feels generous, approachable, and community-minded.
**Probability:** 0.08

## Chosen Direction: Quiet Orchard

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial minimalism with Japanese tea-house restraint and Mediterranean botanical warmth.

### Core Principles
1. Let generous cream space create the feeling of a slow morning rather than a crowded storefront.
2. Pair precise editorial typography with imperfect botanical marks and tactile paper-like surfaces.
3. Use asymmetry and vertical rhythm to make browsing feel like turning through a field journal.
4. Treat every interaction as a small ritual: deliberate, quiet, and rewarding.

### Color Philosophy
The palette is built around oat paper, deep tea leaf, oxidized clay, and a single saffron accent. Warm neutrals make the page feel touchable and grounded; deep green supplies trust and botanical depth; clay adds human warmth; saffron is reserved for moments of invitation and discovery.

### Layout Paradigm
Use a left-anchored editorial spine with offset content blocks, tall image windows, and horizontal rule moments. The page should feel composed like a printed magazine spread, not a centered marketing template. Sections alternate between generous breathing room and compact tasting-note clusters.

### Signature Elements
1. A thin vertical “steep line” motif that marks sections and guides the eye.
2. Small botanical leaf stamps and hand-drawn circles used as accents, never decoration overload.
3. Tasting notes presented as compact specimen labels with serif numerals and precise metadata.

### Interaction Philosophy
Hover states should feel like handling a beautiful object: cards lift slightly, labels reveal a secondary tasting note, and buttons use a firm press response. Mobile navigation opens as a calm full-height paper panel. Interactive filters should never feel like a dashboard.

### Animation
Use short, tactile transitions under 300ms. Hero elements enter with a staggered vertical rise and a slight opacity change. Product cards shift by 4px on hover with a softened shadow. The tea steam illustration uses a slow, low-contrast drift. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` and remove nonessential movement when requested.

### Typography System
Use `DM Serif Display` for expressive headlines and `Manrope` for navigation, metadata, and body copy. Headlines are sentence case with selective italic emphasis; body text uses generous line-height; labels are uppercase with wide tracking; prices and steep times use tabular numerals.

### Brand Essence
Tea for people who want a better pause—small-batch leaves, clear rituals, and no performance. Personality: **quietly confident, observant, warm**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines speak in short, sensory phrases. CTAs are invitations rather than commands. Microcopy is specific, calm, and a little poetic without becoming precious.

Example lines:

> A softer way to start the day.

> Find the cup that meets you where you are.

### Wordmark & Logo
The mark is a simple open teacup formed from two offset crescent leaves, suggesting both a vessel and a pause. The wordmark uses a custom high-contrast serif treatment with a clipped terminal on the final letter, paired with a small vertical rule.

### Signature Brand Color
**Saffron Thread — `#D69A32`**. Used sparingly, it signals warmth, discovery, and the small spark that turns a routine cup into a ritual.
